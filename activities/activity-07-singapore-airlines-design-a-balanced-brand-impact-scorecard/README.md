# Activity 7: Singapore Airlines: Design a Balanced Brand-Impact Scorecard

**Objective:** Evaluate service-brand impact using balanced evidence  
**Expected output:** Eight-metric brand-impact scorecard, KPI dictionary and review agenda  
**Primary source:** https://www.singaporeair.com/en_UK/us/about-us/our-story/

## Goal

Use Singapore Airlines's public service story as the context for a scorecard that connects employee capability, delivery and customer outcomes.

## Real-world scenario

Singapore Airlines publicly describes a longstanding focus on service excellence, innovation and the Singapore Girl as part of its brand story.

A fictional premium regional airline wants to test whether its 'Thoughtful journeys, every time' promise is being delivered consistently across booking, airport, cabin and disruption recovery.

## Evidence boundary

Public case facts and fictional learner data must never be mixed. Every numerical or organisational training input is labelled as an assumption.

### Training assumptions / guardrails

- The airline, promise and dataset are fictional and must not be attributed to Singapore Airlines.
- The mock data includes employee confidence, departure communication, first-contact resolution, effort, satisfaction, complaints and repeat booking.
- The dashboard has space for eight measures and must support a monthly management decision.
- Safety and regulatory measures remain separate hard constraints and cannot be traded for experience scores.

## Questions

1. Which leading and lagging measures best test the promise?
2. How should results be segmented to expose inconsistency?
3. Which metric definitions or data sources could mislead management?
4. What threshold should trigger action, and who owns the response?

## Detailed activity procedure

### Step 1: Define the decision

State the monthly management decision the scorecard must support and the promise outcome being tested.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 2: Build the measurement chain

Connect capability, delivery behaviour, customer response and commercial outcome; identify assumptions.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 3: Select eight measures

Choose a balanced mix of employee, operational, customer and commercial indicators with leading and lagging coverage.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 4: Write KPI definitions

For each measure specify formula, population, source, cadence, owner, target, threshold and limitation.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 5: Analyse mock data

Calculate trends and segment by journey stage, channel and disruption status; identify material differences.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 6: Create the action review

Prioritise one issue, name the decision, owner, due date, expected effect and verification measure.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

## Deliverable

Eight-metric brand-impact scorecard, KPI dictionary and review agenda

## Acceptance criteria

Every KPI has a decision, definition, source, owner and threshold; the set balances four evidence lenses; and the analysis identifies a segment-level action without unsupported causal claims.

## Reflection and debrief questions

1. Which customer or employee insight changed your recommendation most, and why?
2. What evidence would you request before investing or scaling?
3. Which stakeholder owns the next service-brand decision?
4. What should the organisation start, stop or continue?

## Facilitator debrief

Compare recommendations against relevance, distinctiveness, deliverability and evidence quality. Surface unsupported causal claims. Close by naming one decision, one owner and one validation action.
