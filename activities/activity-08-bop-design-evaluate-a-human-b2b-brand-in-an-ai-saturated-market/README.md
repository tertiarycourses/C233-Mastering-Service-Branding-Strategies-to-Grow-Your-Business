# Activity 8: BOP Design: Evaluate a Human B2B Brand in an AI-Saturated Market

**Objective:** Evaluate current service-brand impact and recommend an evidence-based improvement  
**Expected output:** Brand evaluation report and one-page 90-day experiment brief  
**Primary source:** https://www.bopdesign.com/bop-blog/b2b-branding-trends-for-2026-and-2027/

## Goal

Use BOP Design's 2026–2027 trend perspective to build an evaluation plan for a real B2B professional-service context.

## Real-world scenario

BOP Design argues that B2B brands in 2026–2027 need coherent systems, human relevance, strategic AI use, clearer data communication and consistency across machine- and human-mediated discovery.

A Singapore cybersecurity advisory firm has adopted AI-assisted content and lead qualification. Enquiry volume rose, but prospects say the firm feels generic and are less certain whom they will work with.

## Evidence boundary

Public case facts and fictional learner data must never be mixed. Every numerical or organisational training input is labelled as an assumption.

### Training assumptions / guardrails

- The advisory firm, research quotes and funnel data are fictional training inputs, not BOP Design client results.
- The team can run interviews, analyse search/enquiry data and test one website/service-introduction change over 90 days.
- The firm promises 'Clear decisions in complex cyber risk'.
- AI use must preserve confidentiality, accuracy, disclosure and expert accountability.

## Questions

1. Which evidence would distinguish awareness growth from stronger brand preference?
2. How can the firm test whether generic AI-mediated content weakened trust?
3. What human and system cues should be evaluated across the buyer journey?
4. Which 90-day experiment offers the clearest learning at acceptable risk?

## Detailed activity procedure

### Step 1: Frame the evaluation

Define the promise, audience, decision, hypotheses and alternative explanations.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 2: Build the evidence matrix

Combine interviews, journey observation, content/search data, sales feedback, conversion and retention evidence.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 3: Audit measurement quality

Check validity, reliability, timeliness, sample bias, attribution limits and privacy for each source.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 4: Analyse the journey

Compare discovery, evaluation, consultation and proposal stages; segment AI-assisted and expert-led touchpoints.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 5: Diagnose the gap

Identify whether the main issue is relevance, distinctiveness, credibility, consistency or delivery.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 6: Design the experiment

Specify change, audience, comparison, success measures, guardrails, owner and stop/scale decision after 90 days.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

## Deliverable

Brand evaluation report and one-page 90-day experiment brief

## Acceptance criteria

The evaluation triangulates at least three evidence types, states limitations and alternative explanations, and defines an ethical experiment with success, guardrail and stop/scale criteria.

## Reflection and debrief questions

1. Which customer or employee insight changed your recommendation most, and why?
2. What evidence would you request before investing or scaling?
3. Which stakeholder owns the next service-brand decision?
4. What should the organisation start, stop or continue?

## Facilitator debrief

Compare recommendations against relevance, distinctiveness, deliverability and evidence quality. Surface unsupported causal claims. Close by naming one decision, one owner and one validation action.
